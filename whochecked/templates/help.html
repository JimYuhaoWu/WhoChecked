How to read a report

What it does

You give the paper that introduced a resource: a model, a dataset, a tool, from any field. WhoChecked finds that paper in OpenAlex, reads every work OpenAlex records as citing it (up to 10,000), and for each one reports two things: how much it reads like an evaluation, and how many authors it shares with the original paper.

What it does not do

  • It does not say whether the resource is good. It says who checked it and whether they were at arm's length.
  • It does not decide which works are evaluations. The score ranks; it does not classify. A high score means evaluation words, a zero means none were found. Neither is a verdict, so read the works.
  • It does not treat a gap as evidence. A missing author list, a missing abstract or a paper OpenAlex lacks is shown as unknown, never as "not independently evaluated".

Author overlap

The share of a citing work's authors who also wrote the original paper:

  • independent: none shared
  • partial: some, under half
  • same-group: half or more
  • unknown: an author list is missing on one side

Measured on 74 hand-labelled name pairs: 8 of 61 matches joined two different people, and every one of the eight was a match marked weak. A false match counts as overlap, so it makes a resource look less independently evaluated than it is, not more.

Your query

A check runs as a job with a random link. Your query is sent in the form, not the address, so it does not appear in the link, in server access logs, or in what a site you visit next is told. No cookie is set and nothing about you is recorded. The job, query and report included, is deleted {{ retention_days }} days after it was submitted. Anyone you give the link to can open the report until then.
